                                        1      I NTRODUCTION
                                        Speculative decoding reduces the overhead of token-by-token autoregressive inference in large
                                        language models through inexpensive drafting and batched verification. Parallel drafting further
                                        reduces drafting overhead by predicting multiple positions in a single forward pass (Leviathan et al.,
                                        2023; Chen et al., 2023) . DFlash (Chen et al., 2026) adopts this approach to predict an entire masked
                                        draft block in parallel within a single forward pass. Building on this approach, Domino and DSpark
                                        (Huang et al., 2026; Cheng et al., 2026) model dependencies among draft tokens through a GRU state
                                        and transitions conditioned on preceding tokens, respectively, allowing subsequent predictions to
                                        condition on the selected prefix and thereby increasing acceptance length.
                                        However, speculative decoding imposes a key constraint: each verification round can accept only a
                                        contiguous correct prefix starting at the first position of the draft block. Consequently, an error at an
                                        early position prevents subsequent tokens from being committed in the current round, even if those
                                        tokens have been predicted correctly. Moreover, when subsequent predictions are conditioned on an
                                        incorrect token, their conditioning context deviates from the correct prefix, so the early error may
                                        further affect suffix predictions. This creates an inherent limitation for methods such as Domino and
                                        DSpark that introduce additional autoregressive modules: they rely on forward causal dependencies
                                        to improve subsequent choices, but information in later predictions cannot directly revise choices
                                        already made at earlier positions.
                                        In the base draft model, the correct token is often already present in the candidate set when an early
                                        prediction error occurs. Correcting an early selection can therefore enable acceptance of the current
                                        token and potentially a subsequent run of correctly predicted tokens. An underutilized opportunity lies
                                        in backward information flow within the draft block: global information can guide candidate selection

Our central idea is for the entire draft block to serve two roles simultaneously: proposing candidates
at each position and providing context for candidate selection. Because predictions at later positions
are already available before verification, we can identify the most suitable candidate at an earlier
position by comparing candidates’ compatibility with global information from the entire draft block
and with subsequent predictions. Once a candidate is selected, that specific token should condition
subsequent decisions, allowing later choices to follow the actual selected or repaired prefix.
We introduce DRelay, which implements this candidate repair process through the following key
components and techniques: (1) A global reader first integrates candidate distributions across positions
and then performs reads across positions for each candidate to construct a global context tailored
to each alternative token. (2) A causal selector combines global context, the verified target-model
history, and an addressable representation of the selected prefix to sequentially decide whether to
retain the original draft token or select an alternative candidate. (3) We jointly train the backbone
and the selector to learn candidate distributions and repair actions, respectively, and use these two
factors to weight the repair loss, focusing training on choices that contribute more to the accepted
prefix. As shown in Figure 1, DRelay outperforms DFlash, Domino, and DSpark across all displayed
benchmarks on Qwen3-4B, achieving up to a 4.49× end-to-end speedup over autoregressive decoding.
Our main contributions are:

               • We propose DRelay, which uses the entire draft block to guide repairs to early selections
                 and allows subsequent candidates to directly query the actual repaired prefix, completing
                 candidate selection sequentially before verification.
               • We design a joint training objective that simultaneously trains the draft model to propose
                 correct candidates and the selector to make repair decisions, while weighting the repair loss
                 according to prefix value.
               • We evaluate DRelay on Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B across eight math, code, dialogue, and
                 instruction-following benchmarks. Across target models and temperatures, its average
                 end-to-end speedup exceeds that of the best-performing baseline among DFlash, Domino,
                 and DSpark by 7.8–9.9%, while concurrent-serving throughput reaches up to 4.38× that of
                 autoregressive decoding at matched concurrency.


2         R ELATED W ORK

Parallel drafting and causal selection. Speculative decoding amortizes target computation by
verifying several proposals together (Leviathan et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2023). DFlash obtains
parallel proposals through noncausal attention over target-conditioned masked slots (Chen et al.,
2026); DFlare improves how deeper draft layers use target features (Zhang et al., 2026a). Domino
restores within-block dependence with a GRU-conditioned vocabulary residual (Huang et al., 2026).


                                                                2
DSpark uses a preceding-token Markov head by default, offers a recurrent variant, and schedules
verification length according to confidence and load (Cheng et al., 2026). These works establish
the parallel-backbone/causal-head split. DRelay adds an explicit read of the resulting candidate
distributions, then conditions each decision on addressable selected-prefix entries. The distinction is
where selection obtains evidence, rather than whether the backbone has noncausal attention.

Draft-model training and distillation. Target-aligned drafters are trained with supervised or
knowledge-distillation objectives. DistillSpec uses on-policy trajectories and a decoding-aware
divergence (Zhou et al., 2024); EAGLE and EAGLE-3 learn target features through autoregressive
feature prediction and training-time test (Li et al., 2024; 2025). For parallel drafters, DFlash predicts
a masked block in one pass (Chen et al., 2026). Draft-OPD replays verification-exposed errors under
target-assisted rollouts, while D-PACE derives position weights from a differentiable accepted-length
surrogate (Lei et al., 2026; Wu et al., 2026). DRelay jointly trains candidate support and a causal
repair selector, weighting repair by prefix value.

Verifier feedback and prefix-conditioned repair. TAPS scores path reach to select a compact
prefix-closed verification tree (Wang et al., 2026a). Verification-Aware Training predicts cumulative
acceptance and begins a decaying loss schedule at the first rejection (Gu et al., 2026). AdaFlash and
online draft co-training adapt drafters to target-generated data (Qian et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026b).
ReTrace and DFlow reuse rejected-trajectory or verifier information across rounds (Lin et al., 2026;
Zhang et al., 2026b). DRelay instead reads predicted evidence within the current block, alongside
strictly verified target history, and uses that evidence to make addressable repair decisions before
verification.

contiguous prefix starting at the beginning of the block. We investigate an opportunity enabled by

decoding traces from DFlash and Domino with Qwen3-4B (Yang et al., 2025) as the target model,
we analyze this opportunity from three perspectives: candidate availability, information from later
predictions, and gains in accepted prefix length.

3.1   T HE C ORRECT C ANDIDATE U SUALLY R EMAINS AVAILABLE AT THE F IRST E RROR

An early prediction error does not necessarily imply that the correct candidate is absent. As shown
in Figure 2, at the first error in the base predictions, the original top-16 candidate pool contains the
target token in 94.0% of DFlash cases and 95.1% of Domino cases. We separately analyze the first
rejection in Domino’s actual draft sequences and find that the original candidate pool still contains
the target token in 88.8% of cases. These results show that suitable alternatives for repair are already
available at many error positions.


3.2   L ATER P REDICTIONS P ROVIDE D ISCRIMINATIVE S IGNALS FOR E ARLY C ANDIDATE
      S ELECTION

Candidate coverage only indicates whether the correct token is present in the candidate pool; it
does not determine which candidate should be selected when multiple alternatives are available. We
therefore ask whether predictions at later positions in the draft block can provide additional evidence
for an earlier candidate. Intuitively, a correct early candidate should not only be consistent with the
prefix at its own position, but should also support a plausible continuation of the later predictions.
To test this hypothesis, we select samples with an early error for which the correct token is present in
the candidate pool and the next four draft tokens are all correct. For each candidate at the current
position, we treat that candidate as the selected token and use a frozen target model to compute the
total conditional log-probability of the four subsequent draft tokens given the preceding prefix and
the candidate. The candidate with the highest score is considered the candidate supported by the
subsequent predictions. As a control, we replace the four subsequent tokens with a suffix from another
prompt, thereby changing the semantics of the sequence, and compare the resulting token-repair
accuracy. As shown in the middle panel of Figure 2, the real suffix selects the correct candidate in
46/86 DFlash cases and 31/69 Domino cases. Relative to the matched control suffixes, the accuracy
improves by 53.5 and 44.4 percentage points, respectively. Thus, subsequent predictions contain
context-dependent discriminative information that can help repair an early error, rather than merely
providing a suffix unrelated to the preceding candidates.
The entire draft block can therefore serve not only as a source of candidates, but also as global
evidence for candidate selection. DRelay accordingly combines each candidate’s native confidence
with global information from the draft block and the consistency of subsequent predictions. After a
candidate is selected, its token is fed into the subsequent decisions through the selected prefix.


3.3   E ARLY R EPAIR C AN R ECOVER S UBSEQUENT C ORRECT P REDICTIONS

The value of a repair is not limited to the position that it corrects. Let R denote the number of draft
tokens immediately following the first error that are correct. If all other predictions remain unchanged,
correctly repairing this error increases the accepted prefix length by 1 + R: one token comes from
the repaired position and R tokens come from the subsequently recovered consecutive predictions.
To quantify this potential gain, we perform a one-edit oracle experiment. If the target token at the
first rejected position is contained in the original top-16 candidate pool, we replace the erroneous
token with the target token and leave every other position unchanged. As shown in the right panel
of Figure 2, when this gain is averaged over all complete draft blocks and blocks for which the
replacement is unavailable contribute zero, the mean accepted length increases by 1.65 and 1.22
tokens for DFlash and Domino, respectively.
This result shows that the value of a repair should be measured by how much accepted prefix
it enables. DRelay is not restricted to the first error: it evaluates every position and selectively
repairs a position when the global continuation and the selected prefix indicate that the native
draft may be inconsistent. Together, the three observations motivate a selector that reads global
information, performs repairs selectively, and propagates each selected repair to subsequent decisions.
During training, the repair loss should assign greater weight to decisions according to their potential
contribution to the consecutive accepted prefix, improving the prefix length realized at inference
time.

4     DR ELAY: G LOBAL C ANDIDATE R EADS AND C AUSAL S ELECTION
DRelay constructs a candidate path after parallel drafting and before target-model verification. As
shown in Figure 3, DRelay organizes path repair into two modules. Global Read first aggregates
candidate distributions across positions to construct a global context for each candidate. The Causal
Selector then combines this context with the verified history and the selected prefix to decide, at each
position, whether to retain the base prediction or choose an alternative candidate, producing a path
for target-model verification.

4.1    OVERALL ARCHITECTURE AND EXECUTION PROCESS

Figure 3 illustrates a single round of DRelay execution. At the start of each round, the parallel drafter
receives an anchor token confirmed by the target model and historical context features, then generates
hidden states and candidate distributions for subsequent positions in a single forward pass.In Figure 3,
A denotes the anchor token, and M denotes positions to be predicted. The drafter retains the top-16
candidates at each position. The Global Read module then processes all candidates in the block.
The Top-16 slot mixing stage first aggregates the candidate distributions across positions, and the
Candidate reads stage then retrieves information across positions relevant to each candidate.
The contexts produced by these two stages are fused and passed to the Causal Selector. At each
position, the Causal Selector combines the global context, verified history, and currently selected
prefix to decide whether to retain the base prediction or choose an alternative candidate. After
completing candidate selection for the entire block, DRelay submits the resulting single path to the
target model for verification. The target model accepts a contiguous prefix of this path and produces
the anchor token for the next round, after which drafting resumes.

4.3   C AUSAL SELECTOR : MAKE EACH REPAIR PART OF THE CONTEXT

After replacing a token, later choices must use the replacement. An addressable prefix memory
stores the anchor and each selected entry, whose key and value encode (hj , zjglobal , E(xj ), ga , j).
At position i, a candidate can retrieve only entries j < i; the latest chosen token E(xi−1 ) also
conditions its query directly. This exposes earlier choices individually instead of compressing them
into one recurrent state. A separate candidate-specific read of the last 64 strictly verified target
features provides direct access to established context instead of relying only on its compression into
draft states. Because this history does not change within the block, all its reads are computed before
selection.
The Rank branch combines native log-probabilities with a shared hidden residual and compact
candidate-specific corrections from global, history, and prefix evidence. Its scores Sik rank nonbase
alternatives. The Gate branch asks a different question: whether the best available repair justifies
leaving the native choice. This conservative gate is intended to correct likely error positions while
minimizing changes to tokens that are already consistent with the global context and selected prefix.

gradients pass through draft features and memory projections. Teacher proposal tokens never enter

7         C ONCLUSION

DRelay treats a parallel draft as evidence for its own token choices. Native-draft diagnostics connect
three opportunities: early mistakes often retain the correct candidate, reliable later predictions
help distinguish that candidate, and a precise repair can recover a consecutive continuation. The
architecture follows this logic by reading candidate-level global context once, then making selective
repairs against the actual selected prefix. Joint support and repair learning aligns these decisions with
prefix reach and continuation value. The system results quantify the end-to-end trade-off, while the
latency breakdown identifies the cost of the selector relative to verification and drafting.

A    R EFERENCE D IAGNOSTICS
This appendix defines the offline diagnostics used to motivate DRelay in Section 3. They are computed
from frozen decoding traces and are separate from the trained-system evaluation in Section 6. In
particular, the diagnostics measure whether useful evidence is present in a parallel draft; they do not
use oracle labels as inputs to DRelay at inference time.

Reference traces and terminology. We collect native parallel-drafting traces from DFlash and
Domino (Chen et al., 2026; Huang et al., 2026) with Qwen3-4B as the target model (Yang et al.,
2025). At each position, the base token is the backbone argmax and the served token is the token
selected by the corresponding native drafting procedure. The target’s greedy continuation under
the verified prefix supplies the reference token used to identify a mismatch. The original top-16
candidate pool is retained for the candidate-availability analyses. All traces use the same tokenizer
and verification rule as the corresponding main-text diagnostic.

Candidate coverage. For a position i, let yi be the target reference token and let Vi be the original
candidate pool. We record ci = ⊮[yi ∈ Vi ]. Coverage is evaluated at the first base error and at the
first error in the native served path, with the latter restricted to positions whose served pool is defined.
These strata localize where a repair is possible; they do not imply that a deployed selector can observe
the target token or the error location.

Continuation evidence. For a candidate v ∈ Vi , we score a later predicted continuation under the
verified prefix extended by v. The score uses the target model’s conditional likelihood of the predicted
tokens after position i and omits the current-token target probability. Replacing the continuation by a
correctness-matched continuation from another prompt provides the control comparison in Figure 2.
The control changes the future content while preserving the task and reliability strata, so a positive
real-minus-control contrast indicates context-aligned evidence rather than candidate prior alone.

One-edit repair value. Let A be the number of proposals accepted before the first served error and
let R be the number of immediately following proposals that agree with the target. If the target token
is covered, changing only that error increases the accepted prefix by 1 + R; later matches separated
by another error do not count. We report this quantity as an oracle opportunity analysis over complete
blocks. It is not an estimate of DRelay’s realized gain, because DRelay must infer both whether to
edit and which candidate to select.

Interpretation and scope. The diagnostics establish the existence of candidate availability and
continuation evidence that a compact selector may exploit. They do not provide supervision from
unverified target states, do not evaluate a learned repair policy, and do not replace the end-to-end
speed and acceptance results reported in Section 6.

B    I MPLEMENTATION AND T RAINING D ETAILS
This appendix records the implementation contract shared by the reported experiments. The equations
in Sections 4 and 5 give the full computation; the points below make explicit the information
boundaries and supervision used in the reported configuration.

Information boundary. At each round the parallel drafter produces hidden representations and
a candidate distribution for each of H = 15 positions. DRelay retains the native top-K pool with
K = 16. Global Read consumes only these draft-side representations and candidate distributions. It
never reads a teacher proposal token or a future token that has not been verified by the target model.
The Causal Selector receives the resulting candidate-specific global features, the strictly verified
history, and a prefix memory whose entry at position j is visible only to decisions at positions i > j.
Once a candidate is selected, it is the only token written into the prefix used by later decisions in the
same block.

Trainable and frozen components. The target model, token embedding, and vocabulary readout
remain frozen. The parallel drafting backbone, Global Read, and Causal Selector are trained jointly.

Supervision. For a target token yi , the dense support term trains the draft distribution to place

Training and serving protocol. We train on the complete PerfectBlend corpus through SpecForge,

model, candidate budget, decoding budget, and evaluation hardware. During serving, Global Read is
computed once per parallel draft and is followed by the H causal selector decisions; the target then
verifies the resulting single path and supplies the next anchor. Thus the reported latency includes both
the shared draft/verification work and the method-specific selection overhead shown in Figure 4.

C    D IAGNOSTIC S COPE AND I NTERPRETATION
The motivation analysis asks whether later parallel predictions contain information that can distinguish
candidates at an earlier position. This appendix clarifies the diagnostic boundary used for that
question.

What is scored. For a candidate v at position i, the offline assessor conditions on the verified prefix
followed by v and scores only the later predicted draft tokens. The current target-token probability is
excluded, so the score measures continuation compatibility rather than simply reproducing the native
candidate prior. Real continuations are compared with correctness-matched continuations from other
prompts. The target model is used as an offline assessor for this measurement; it is not queried for
unverified future states by DRelay’s Global Read.

What the diagnostic does not claim. First-error localization and correctness filters are used only to
form interpretable offline strata. They are not available to the deployed selector, and they do not define
an inference-time repair rule. Likewise, an oracle one-edit gain measures the value of a possible
correction under the original candidate pool; it is not an end-to-end speedup or an accepted-length
prediction for the trained system.

Connection to DRelay. The diagnostic supports the design choice of reading global candidate
context while retaining a causal prefix selector. Section 4 implements that boundary using draft-side
hidden states, candidate distributions, and verified history, and Section 6 measures the resulting
trained system on all eight benchmarks. The appendix therefore serves as a definition of the evidence
used in the motivation, rather than as a second set of model or benchmark results.
